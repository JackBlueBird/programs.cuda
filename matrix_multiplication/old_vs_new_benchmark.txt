OLD vs NEW cuBLAS SGEMM benchmark
=================================

Files
  gpu_performance_cuda.pdf   old benchmark (matrix_multiply.cu)
  benchmark_example.png      new benchmark (matrix_multiply_REDONE.cu), with and without charger

SGEMM, GFLOP/s, computation + data transfer (new: laptop with charger)

  N       old     new     new / old
  500       4     543     x136
  1000     33    1267     x38
  1500    102    1808     x18
  2000    232    2279     x10
  2500    406    2817     x7
  3000    683    3167     x4.6
  3500    974    3358     x3.4

Why the old numbers are so low
  - The old program timed ONE call, the FIRST one, with no warm up.
  - The first cuBLAS call pays the library initialization: a fixed cost of tens of ms.
  - At small N the real computation takes less than 1 ms, so the old time was almost all initialization.
    The fixed cost weighs less as N grows: the ratio goes from x136 down to x3.4.
  - Proof inside the old plot: STRMM looks faster than SGEMM at every N. It was called AFTER SGEMM,
    when cuBLAS was already initialized, so it got the warm up that SGEMM had paid for.

Other differences
  - Correctness: the old SGEMM call (CUBLAS_OP_T, CUBLAS_OP_T) returned C transposed; with constant
    matrices it could not be seen. The new one uses random matrices and is verified against the CPU.
  - Statistics: old = 1 measurement; new = warm up + average of several iterations.
  - Range: old up to N = 3500; new up to N = 15000, where the plateau (GPU power limit) shows up.
  - New also measures computation only (~5.3 TFLOP/s with charger, ~3.7 on battery at large N).
  - Hardware of the old plot not recorded (compile notes suggest sm_86, maybe the cluster):
    part of the difference could come from a different GPU.

Lesson
  Always warm up before timing a GPU library. A single detail of method underestimated cuBLAS
  by up to 100 times.
